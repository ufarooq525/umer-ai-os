# Global Opportunity Intelligence

## Mission

The OS continuously searches for economically valuable problems anywhere in the world and determines whether they can become:

- a service client
- a repeatable service
- a software/product opportunity
- a partnership
- an acquisition or other commercial opportunity

The system is **not** a website lead generator and is **not** permanently constrained to predefined niches.

## Discovery model

```text
Global signals
    ↓
Markets / industries / geographies / niches
    ↓
Businesses + people + workflows
    ↓
Problems / demand / inefficiencies
    ↓
Evidence collection
    ↓
Opportunity formation
    ↓
Commercial + delivery scoring
    ↓
Experiment
    ↓
Service / product / partnership / reject
    ↓
Revenue + learning
    ↓
Updated global search strategy
```

## Discovery lenses

### Vertical

- country
- region
- industry
- niche
- business type

### Horizontal

- quoting
- scheduling
- booking
- payments
- inventory
- compliance
- document processing
- reporting
- data entry
- customer communication
- CRM workflows
- integrations
- portals
- repetitive operations

The system should intersect both dimensions. A problem can be more valuable than the niche in which it was first discovered.

## Signal families

The scanner may investigate authorized public sources such as:

- business websites and directories
- search results
- public reviews
- social/business pages
- job postings
- public forums and communities
- product/pricing pages
- company announcements
- public documentation and changelogs
- public market/regulatory information

Source-specific terms, access limits and applicable laws must be respected.

## High-value signals

Examples include:

- repeated customer complaints
- repeated pre-sales questions
- manual quotation workflows
- spreadsheet-heavy operations
- email/WhatsApp-based workflows
- businesses hiring for repetitive operational work
- fragmented systems
- obvious integration gaps
- outdated digital sales infrastructure
- strong demand but weak conversion paths
- new regulation creating compliance work
- fast-growing markets with immature software
- repeated requests for unavailable capabilities

A signal is not automatically a problem. It becomes useful only after validation.

## Opportunity score

Opportunity scoring should combine:

- problem severity
- frequency/demand
- financial impact
- ability to pay
- reachability
- competitive gap
- solution fit
- delivery feasibility
- evidence confidence
- time to revenue
- expected margin

The exact weights should be configurable and learned from historical outcomes.

## Experiment-driven validation

The OS should create bounded experiments instead of researching indefinitely.

Example:

```text
Hypothesis:
Saudi private Umrah transport operators have a meaningful
commercial need for better digital quotation/booking.

Sample:
50 businesses

Success criteria:
- sufficient recurring problem evidence
- opportunity score threshold reached
- qualified decision makers reachable
- positive commercial response
- acceptable expected deal economics

Decision:
scale / change offer / continue testing / reject
```

## Opportunity classes

### Service

A specific business has a problem worth solving for a project fee.

### Repeatable service

The same problem appears repeatedly and can be packaged into an offer.

### Product

The same problem is sufficiently common and valuable across a market that a product may outperform project work.

### Other

Partnership, acquisition, referral, data/service, or another validated commercial path.

## Portfolio logic

Do not optimize only for maximum theoretical deal size.

The OS should balance:

- immediate revenue
- expected revenue
- time to revenue
- confidence
- research cost
- delivery effort
- margin
- strategic product potential

A smaller highly reachable opportunity can outrank a huge but speculative one.

## Kill rules

The OS must be able to recommend stopping.

A market or hypothesis should be paused/rejected when evidence shows poor problem density, weak willingness to pay, poor reachability, excessive competition, unattractive economics, or consistently poor experiment outcomes.

Stopping research is a successful outcome when it preserves resources.

## Learning loop

Every meaningful commercial result becomes feedback:

```text
Hypothesis
  ↓
Research
  ↓
Experiment
  ↓
Sales result
  ↓
Delivery result
  ↓
Observed economics
  ↓
Model update
  ↓
New search priorities
```

## Non-negotiable traceability

Every important recommendation must be traceable:

```text
Recommendation
  → Opportunity
  → Problem
  → Observation
  → Evidence
  → Source
```

Every important score must expose the factors and confidence behind it.
